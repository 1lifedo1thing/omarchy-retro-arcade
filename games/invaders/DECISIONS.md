# Implementation decisions for review

1. Working title: **Omarchy Invaders**. Space Invaders-inspired play with original Rust rules and hand-authored pixel sprites. No upstream game engine, ROM or copied game assets. For this small deterministic game, a standalone Rust core gives simpler packaging than adapting an existing C/C++ game.
2. Fixed 120 Hz simulation; rendering scales an 800×700 field without changing collision geometry. Three lives, eleven columns and five rows. Holding fire is supported, with at most three player shots.
3. Four bunkers erode under either side's fire. New waves restore bunkers; lives carry over. Difficulty growth is capped. No online leaderboard, account, telemetry or multiplayer in this milestone.
4. Score tiers: 30/20/10 by row, bonus craft 150. No random bonus multipliers. Deterministic internal random sequence makes a fresh run reproducible; this is not a competitive anti-cheat system.
5. Native eframe/egui front end, following Chess's menus and shortcuts. Focus loss pauses. Resuming is explicit. Saves resume paused. New-game confirmation archives prior state.
6. Follow Omarchy by default; charcoal playfield remains dark even with a light desktop. Very dark accents fall back to sage in the playfield so enemies remain visible. Desktop files are never modified.
7. Sound off by default. Optional synthesized impact cues, inherited helper from Chess. A full arcade soundtrack and richer effects remain future polish.
8. One atomic local JSON file contains the run, high score and preferences; five-second autosave and normal-exit save. Lifetime file lock. Invalid data is preserved and requires an explicit recovery action.
9. GPL-3.0-or-later. Arch package plus standalone Linux CI binary. Rust remains the application language. No Python runtime or build dependency.
10. Artwork is an initial playable pixel direction. Visual approval and real Omarchy playtesting remain outstanding; tests passing do not establish finished game feel.

## Design exploration — 11 September 2026 (unapproved)

Three image-generated concept sheets were presented in this display order: 1. Super Nova Press; 2. Foundry Orbit; 3. Night Garden. These are visual targets for discussion, not implemented artwork, production sprite sheets or verified gameplay. No direction is selected. Each was requested with the same 860×900 gameplay window and accompanying sprite, damage, icon and UI-state studies; generated framing varies slightly, so these are not pixel-exact layout specifications.

- Super Nova Press: flat print-inspired silhouettes, ivory arena, charcoal player shots and vermilion hostile darts. Punchy two-pose movement, recoil and brief ink-burst destruction. Requires faithfully authored textured sprite animation, typography and bunker damage masking; no 3D renderer needed. The light arena is a proposed exception to decision 6, conditional on selection. Texture must stay quieter than threats.
- Foundry Orbit: ceramic/enamel spacecraft with machined joints, articulated drones and fractured blast shelters. Recoil, clamp gait and small metal-fragment explosions. Requires authored painted or prerendered animation atlases, controlled highlights, damage masking and bounded particles. It does not inherently require real-time 3D. Principal risk: fine mechanical detail disappearing at small size; keep the distinctive sculpted forms rather than replacing them with basic polygons.
- Night Garden: illustrated mechanical insects and seed-shell shelters, subdued botanical border, ivory needles and coral hostile thorns. Wing cycles, shell recoil and petal-like fracture. Requires authored transparent animation atlases, layered shell artwork, damage masking and restrained effects. Principal risks: thin wings at small scale and foliage competing with threats; test both explicitly.

Shared proposed constraints: preserve simulation, formation rules, scoring and collision geometry. Store replaceable player/enemy art separately from rules, keyed by entity role with frame timing, visual bounds and anchor metadata; collision bounds remain authoritative in the simulation. Draw projectile cores over decorative particles. Shape and luminance must distinguish threats independently of hue; constrain theme substitutions accordingly. Pause on focus loss remains mandatory; pause is explicit-resume, wave treatments briefly occupy the clear arena, and game-over preserves the final score with keyboard retry. Match the chosen ship/icon artwork in About. Keep existing Arcade menus, sound defaults and persistence.

Generated-sheet errors are not design decisions: altered formation counts, bonus craft duplicated into formation rows, invented enemy durability/speed captions, inconsistent row assignments, slogans and incidental text must not change game rules. Ship and enemy art would be cleaned and animated after selection, then compared with the approved concept in actual 860×900 and compact renders. Report deviations explicitly; passing tests do not prove artwork fidelity. ### Selected direction and formation revision

Tom selected Foundry Orbit and requested that the invaders start in the shape of the Omarchy brand name. Orbit is the selected art direction; the revised wordmark formation is still a concept awaiting review. Use the official wordmark silhouette as the formation reference, with individual enemies occupying its strokes and empty letter counters preserved. Proposed movement: march together, leaving holes where enemies are destroyed; do not refill destroyed slots. This intentionally changes the earlier 11×5 formation decision. Enemy count, sprite size, firing density, wave duration and collision spacing require tuning after approval, not assumptions that the old balance still holds. The visual challenge is preserving recognisable ships while fitting seven letters and enough horizontal marching room. No implementation or production asset creation in this revision.

### Orbit pixel-art revision

Tom found the Orbit wordmark concept too polished and requested a more 8-bit game. Retain Orbit ship identities and the OMARCHY enemy formation, but explore deliberately low-resolution sprites, a limited palette, flat shading, bitmap score typography, two-frame marching poses and compact square-fragment explosions. Remove smooth material rendering, bloom and detailed scenery. This supersedes the earlier preference for painted/prerendered mechanical material detail; it does not approve arbitrary simplified redraws. The new pixel concept must be reviewed before implementation. Pixel sprite scale and formation legibility remain the main trade-off.

### 8-bit Orbit implementation

Tom approved the 8-bit study and explicitly requested implementation. The game now uses the generated, palette-normalised PNG atlas rather than a procedural ship redraw. Frames retain the forked interceptor, clamp eye, split sentry and four-lobed carrier. A separate generated station texture supplies subdued corner scenery. Bitmap score and state labels, nearest-neighbour sprite sampling, alternating march poses and 0.36-second explosion sequences establish the pixel presentation.

The opening formation uses seven 3×5 letter masks with 24-unit column spacing and 28-unit row spacing. This is a deliberately simplified spelling, not an exact official wordmark reproduction: an exact dense wordmark would require much smaller sprites. New enemy hitboxes are 20×22 units independently of sprite data; old saves retain their prior geometry until the next wave. Firing frequency stays bounded independently of enemy count, and formation march timing is adjusted for the larger population. Real-world difficulty tuning remains required.

Differences from the concept: formation sprites are smaller to keep seven letters and side margins; bunker shading is flattened into three colour bands tied to physical damage; the wordmark is simplified; menus retain native accessible text rather than bitmap lettering. Artwork reload validates file size, dimensions and alpha, preserves the previous image on error, and never resets gameplay. Custom selection persists; the copy button never overwrites an existing file. Game-over now supports Enter retry. Launcher and About use the same player artwork.

### Mouse controls in Arcade (#6)

Pointer movement over the owned playfield sets a horizontal target; the ship follows at its existing 340 units/second, without teleporting or overshooting. Holding the primary button fires only when the press began in the field. Keyboard movement clears the mouse target; stationary pointer position does not take control back. Menus, dialogs, loss of focus, pause and game over block mouse gameplay. Opening the Game menu suspends simulation while it is open; selecting Pause/resume can resume after it closes. Save schemas, physics and artwork are unchanged.
